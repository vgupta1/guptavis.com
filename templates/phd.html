Two Separate PhD Programs

I advise in two separate PhD programs. In either one, you could work with me on almost exactly the same research questions. What differs is everything around the research: the qualifying exams, the problems your peers are working on, and what counts as success at the end. Both guarantee five years of funding with satisfactory progress. Apply to either or both.

The ORAI Certificate and Fellowships

Whichever program you apply to, I strongly encourage my students to complete the ORAI PhD Certificate, which is open to students in either program.

I have dedicated fellowship funding for students who commit to the ORAI certificate and are US citizens or permanent residents. I especially encourage such applicants. I also have research funding without citizenship restrictions.

Who I’m Looking For

I’m looking for students who want to work at the intersection of operations research and AI, particularly on how to leverage AI tools in data-driven decision-making.

Math comes first. I look for students who think clearly and have some background in proof-based mathematics. Now that AI tools write much of the code, being able to program or call a few ML packages isn’t enough for the research I do. If you want heavily computational ML work without digging into the underlying mathematics, a different advisor will be a better fit. I believe deep understanding is the goal of scholarship.

What Working With Me Looks Like

I’ve written down what I expect from my students and what they can expect from me: how we run meetings, how I think about effort and curiosity, using AI, internships and careers, and taking care of yourself. Read Advising Expectations for You and for Me before you apply.

How I Think About AI in Research

AI eliminates the tedium, but not the thinking.

I use AI tools every day, and I expect my students to as well: to check algebra, work through a proof, or quickly get oriented in a new paper. AI eliminates the tedium, but not the thinking. The point of research is substantive human insight: understanding why something is true, not only that it is. The students who do best with me treat these tools like a sharp colleague. They ask follow-up questions, push back on answers, and steer toward directions of their own.

When I talk with prospective students, expect me to ask you to walk me through a paper you’ve read or a result you’ve proved, in your own words.

If You Do Email

You don’t need to email me to apply. If you do write, keep it short and in your own words, and name one specific thing in one of my papers that you found interesting or didn’t understand, and why. I can’t respond to general inquiries.
